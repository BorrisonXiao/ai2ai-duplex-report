What to think

The plan in six sentences

S1 is the duplex speech model; S2 is the reasoning model that can support it.

  • Start with S1 and validate the evaluation pipeline on real model outputs before running more experiments.
  • Use all 105 distinct questions for the final evaluation, after checking every reference answer.
  • Use shared 10–40 second chunks for amortized thinking, while keeping each S1 model’s native audio frames.
  • Compare S1-only responses with reactive S2 and S2 that reuses results generated while listening.
  • Measure answer accuracy and request-time thinking tokens, and count earlier work separately.
  • Keep the progress report, examples and checklists synchronized as runs finish.

Amortized thinking

  • Reason while listening, then save and reuse the results when a user asks a question.
  • The term describes moving and reusing work before a request; it does not establish lower total compute or latency, which must be measured.
  • No model training is implied; the current plan reuses inference results.

This name fits the project: earlier work may support later requests. Chronological Thinking also describes reasoning during listening as amortized; our proposed experiments still need to test the benefit.

Use shared chunks with enough speech

Small S1 audio frames stay model-specific. The larger amortized thinking chunks are shared across text and audio conditions; voice activity detection (VAD) marks speech and pauses.

  • Use at least 10 elapsed seconds and eight seconds of voiced speech; close early after an observed two-second pause, otherwise close at the first observed pause of at least 0.5 seconds after the 25-second target, with a hard cap at 40 seconds.
  • Short backchannels and fillers are accumulated with surrounding speech rather than becoming their own calls; a long low-information block may return NO_UPDATE, with its tokens counted.
  • At 40 seconds, close at the current audio frame without looking ahead; mark continuing speech as partial and let the next chunk include its continuation.
  • A capped interval with less than eight seconds of speech is a passive chunk: keep all its samples in history but skip a thinking call; the next active call still has that earlier audio available.
  • At request onset, stop launching amortized thinking. Put the unclosed meeting tail and the complete request into the final input, even if the tail is shorter than the normal minimum.
  • Use one causal audio-VAD boundary manifest across GT text, both ASR tools and speech S2; no GT sentence boundaries or question-specific evidence may choose the cuts.
  • A pause is used only after its silence has been observed; text prefixes include only already available words, never the complete future part of an intersecting utterance.
  • Macro chunks and native S1 frames cover the same original samples, request and source horizon; preserve an interval/hash ledger with no dropped or duplicated speech.

Keep all development settings within the user-approved 10–40 second range; compare 20-, 25- and 30-second targets, then freeze one schedule. The schedule is fixed across all conditions; learned semantic trigger timing is deferred.

Separate time, instructions and results

For one evaluation case e, times are in seconds and the case subscript is omitted from within-case chunk symbols (i, t_i, X_i, z_i), transcripts (Y) and prompts (Q); e always identifies a question, while m always identifies a model. GT means the manual ground-truth transcript; ASR means automatic speech recognition. The actual user question is part of the input at request time; it is hidden from all earlier model calls.

Example: \(e=17\) means the seventeenth question; \(m\) could identify Qwen3-30B Instruct; \(i=3\) is its third larger thinking chunk, while \(j\) counts the model’s much smaller input frames.

The experiment families

A reactive S2 baseline runs only after the actual request. An amortized thinking variant has two parts: updates while listening and a final request-time answer.

\[z_i\sim p_{\theta_m}(z_i\mid D_{e,i},Z_{<i},Q_{\le i}^{(g)})\]

Apply this update only to chunks ending before the request: \(t_i<S_e\). Here \(D_{e,i}\) is the available GT transcript, generated transcript or audio prefix. Collect only those earlier results in \(Z_e^{-}\); the unfinished meeting tail and complete request are added to the final input.

For a model without a native thinking channel, the output is only \(r_e\); omit \(h_e\) from the joint expression. These equations describe inference behavior, not a new training loss or a measured log-likelihood. Supplying earlier results does not make them correct by definition.

  • This is an unpaced content study: complete each thinking chunk call before releasing the next chunk; measure accuracy and token movement, without a real-time latency claim.
  • Amortized thinking branches see only earlier original meeting speech; synthetic evaluation questions and answers never enter the shared earlier meeting history.
  • At the request, use the whole available history plus retained earlier results; user-request recognition in generated-transcript conditions comes from that tool, with a GT-question-text diagnostic reported separately.
  • Use the same final-answer instruction and within-model decoding/output allowance across paired conditions.
  • Full earlier reasoning text and saved notes are retained when possible; chat-template stripping of previous thinking must be checked using actual model inputs.
  • Track each transcript/speaker revision at its release; do not use full-meeting future diarization or oracle speaker-name mappings in generated-transcript conditions.
  • Fork independent question examples from the original meeting history; earlier synthetic questions or model replies never become history for later examples.
  • S2 audio experiments produce text answers and earlier results; native speech is scored in S1. Qualify any S2 engine that cannot separate text/reasoning from speech output.
  • Reuse causal earlier results per meeting/model/tool/prompt where their exact inputs match, without exposing question times to the worker.
  • Choose generation limits on development data: start with 8192 new tokens for native Thinking models and 4096 for Instruct, raise if needed for complete outputs, then freeze within-model paired limits and report all length stops.
  • The appended GT request text must match the chosen recorded question, including any retained alternative; every condition uses the same spoken request and gold intent.
  • After the request, allow only the fixed silence/EOS needed by native speech decoding, never resumed future meeting audio; save the full output and select scoring spans by timing rather than correctness.

Draft instructions, outputs and controls

You are doing amortized thinking before any user request. Use only the supplied past conversation and earlier machine-generated results. Earlier results can be wrong: source evidence overrides them. Do not speak to participants or invent a future question. Preserve uncertainty and source references. Follow the selected goal and return a compact set of notes. If nothing useful changed, return NO_UPDATE.

For text: Cite supplied utterance IDs; source quotations are copied by code later rather than regenerated by the model.

For audio: Use only the supplied audio and chunk/time identifiers; do not assume a ground-truth transcript or speaker names. Mark inaudible or uncertain content.

Shared final-answer instruction: Answer the actual user request from the available conversation. Give a concise supported answer, and state what the conversation does not establish. This answer-format instruction is identical across paired conditions.

Supply earlier traces and saved notes as explicit read-only machine-generated context. Verify that chat formatting preserves them. Native continuation/preserve-thinking settings are recorded separately and are not silently assumed.

Combined-goal notes can contain facts, person/task links, checked implications, open items and source references; the summary control remains a summary rather than being forced into all these jobs.

  • Same goal only at the actual request, with no earlier traces, to measure the prompt contribution.
  • Earlier earlier results removed while keeping the final-answer instruction fixed, to test reuse.
  • Compact saved notes versus full earlier traces as a separately labeled memory ablation.

Accuracy and thinking tokens for the request

  • Primary accuracy: a complete source-supported answer without a material unsupported claim, judged using verified facts, rules and a blinded LLM judge.
  • Primary token measure: newly generated native thinking after the actual request, excluding all earlier amortized thinking and answer text.
  • For absent native thinking channels use not-applicable; for unexposed channels use unavailable, never a fabricated zero or a forced chain of thought.
  • Also list final-answer tokens, total request-time output, request-time input text tokens, earlier amortized thinking output, truncations and context mode.
  • Report native thinking means over all eligible attempts, correct-only values separately, and accuracy alongside every token comparison; silence and failed answers cannot win on token count.
  • Use model-specific tokenizers and compare token reductions primarily within the same model; different tokenizers and input costs prevent a universal compute interpretation.
  • Have the judge see the request, verified facts/source evidence and candidate answer, with model and condition names hidden; inspect disputes directly against the source.
  • For S1, independently transcribe generated audio and inspect disputed words/numbers by listening; an internal text stream is diagnostic, not a substitute for the spoken answer.
  • Report per-question logical amortized thinking exposure and the actual unique executed amortized thinking totals separately, so reuse of a shared earlier meeting history is not double-counted.
  • A helper or selector that uses the actual request performs request-time work: count its native reasoning and ordinary output, never relabel it amortized thinking. The main history selector uses fixed rules.

Retain full history, then use a declared fallback

  • Preflight audio duration, encoded positions, text length, prior-trace length and output reserve before each family.
  • Try full history first; reducing concurrency or a qualified larger allocation may resolve memory pressure without changing the model context limit.
  • When full history cannot fit, retain the request, recent complete chunks and useful earlier results; preserve older commitments, revisions and source anchors with a fixed rule using available information only.
  • For a paired comparison, use the same selected raw-history intervals and output reserve; list which earlier results were retained or removed.
