How this batch works

Who does what? Codex selects sources, writes some questions and reviews the results. Local Qwen models generate the other questions and answer questions from transcripts as a quality check. The meeting assistant is the duplex model that would hear and answer the inserted question.

Earlier pilots depended heavily on Codex selecting evidence and writing comparison questions. This batch lets the local model work from the complete available transcript and generate up to three distinct questions at each cutoff. There is no question quota to fill when a useful request does not fit.

A retained question must sound like a plausible request from that participant, have a supported premise and answer, and require a fact from more than a minute earlier. Codex checks source passages rather than accepting a model’s approval flag. Multiple citations or failure with only the latest minute do not prove multi-hop reasoning. Generation and automated screening use the same model; independent human ratings remain a separate step.

Which meetings and times?

The selection covers new participant groups, technical discussions, annotation work, office planning and remote-control design. It deliberately varies topics and design stages; it is not a random sample. The new participant groups do not overlap with each other or the five earlier AMI meetings. Earlier sessions are never supplied as context for a later session.

Targets are five, seven-and-a-half and ten minutes, the midpoint and 80% of the meeting. The actual time uses a gap after completed speech. 98 of 100 prepared cutoffs fit the declared 49,152-token context with the output allowance; two long cutoffs were omitted, without truncating their history. Timing fractions use official AMI meeting durations.

Model and resources

Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 runs through vLLM 0.16.0 in BF16, with one GPU, a 49,152-token context, 8 GiB of KV cache, prefix caching, CUDA graphs and batches of up to four requests. One resident engine handles sequential generation and screening stages. No TTS model, voice encoder or duplex service shares the allocation.
