1. Results on 105 questions

The same 105 questions from 26 meetings are evaluated in every condition. 2,100/2,100 planned answers are saved. Counts include empty final outputs; these remain evaluation cases. All 2,100 outputs have scores, and the specified source checks are complete.

Accuracy status: Source-reviewed responses are reused by exact response text. Other automatic labels remain provisional, including after targeted source checks finish.

Empty final answers: Qwen3-30B Instruct: 0; Qwen3-30B Thinking: 2; MiniCPM-o 4.5: 65; Qwen3-Omni Thinking: 13. These answers are scored incorrect, and their generated tokens remain in the cost totals.

Table 1 reports each seed separately, with 105 questions in every row. A seed initializes random sampling. Instruct uses one greedy run with seed 23; Thinking has separate sampling runs for seeds 23 and 47. The later paired analyses match the same question and seed, then combine both Thinking runs.

Reading the text conditions: Reactive and all three saved-material conditions receive the same complete available speaker-labelled reference transcript. Source quotes repeat selected passages from that transcript in the extra saved material; the writer chose their source IDs before the question, without a question-based search. See the selection process and a worked example.

2. Request-time thinking and complete cost

Request-time thinking tokens are generated after the participant asks the question. Earlier note generation is work performed before the question and is counted separately; the shared text writer generates note edits without a separate exposed thinking channel. Native reasoning-token counts are reported for Qwen Thinking and Qwen Omni. MiniCPM thinking mode is disabled in these runs; Instruct has no separate thinking channel. Figure 1 includes all earlier note work once per meeting and speaker condition, including unused updates and rejected operations. Figure 2 tests paired changes relative to reactive answering; its label n gives the number of valid paired outputs. First-text timing includes incorrect outputs; accuracy is reported alongside it.

For text Thinking, quotes plus earlier evidence reduce mean request-time thinking by 171 tokens, while the median paired change is 40.5 more tokens. The uncertainty interval includes zero. Among the 180 pairs whose two answers both receive fully supported labels, mean thinking instead increases by 34 tokens. The lower overall mean therefore does not describe a typical reduction across questions.

For Qwen audio, function notes increase first-text time by 3.03 seconds with audio alone, and by 2.23 seconds with labelled voice examples. Both time intervals lie above zero, and this slower onset also occurs in pairs whose two answers are fully supported. The accuracy changes run in opposite directions across the two speaker conditions; both accuracy intervals include zero.

MiniCPM onset timing

With audio alone, MiniCPM first text starts 0.09 seconds earlier on average across 76 matched timing pairs, while the median change is nearly zero. Fully supported labels change from 42 to 41 of 105. Its thinking mode is disabled, so this is an onset measurement alongside answer quality.

3. Periodic conversation overviews: eight-question pilot

  • All 608 evaluation records are saved and source-checked across eight questions from five meetings.
  • Text Thinking shows no joint improvement in answer quality and first-answer time over Reactive.
  • With audio alone, one Qwen overview setting matches detailed notes at 7/8 and reduces request-time thinking by 17.2%, while adding earlier generation cost.
  • MiniCPM frequently repeats the question or produces corrupted text, so its short onset times give little evidence of useful answering.
  • The new overview methods have not yet been evaluated on all 105 questions.

Every pilot row evaluates the same eight questions at seed 23. The 480 main evaluations cover text and audio conditions; a further 128 text evaluations check input order, and 20 source-only probes inspect overview generation. Reused controls appear in these matched comparisons without being counted as new questions. These are descriptive development results; the earlier 105-question tables retain their published scope and labels.

Inputs: Reactive receives the available history and the question; Detailed notes adds the current function-call notes; overview conditions add a compact analysis of the broad topic, current discussion, speakers and, for audio, optional voice delivery or tentative emotion. Every condition retains the same raw history within its model and speaker condition. The text writer receives new reference-transcript segments, recent detailed facts and its previous overview; audio writers receive the corresponding new audio, audio-derived facts and previous overview. The final text prompt places the question last.

Update period: every 2, 4 or 8 processed chunks corresponds to mean intervals of 60.5, 121.0 or 241.8 seconds in the frozen streams. Chunk boundaries are unchanged; no update is triggered by the future question. Versioned function calls retain earlier changes while the current overview is limited to 768 tokens.

Pilot scoring, cost accounting and execution scope
  • Codex checked every distinct final answer against the complete past reference transcript and the frozen minimum-fact checklist; exact same-question answers reuse that decision, and empty final answers count as failures.
  • Fully supported correctness requires all requested facts and no material unsupported additions; source-location errors and minor non-verbatim paraphrases are recorded separately when the answer content remains correct.
  • The frozen poster-session checklist requires the earlier 36-talk maximum, the revised 30-talk plan and a half-day poster allocation; the browser separately states whether the answer gets the 36-to-30 comparison right.
  • Two reused poster-answer strings had earlier source labels that accepted counts alone; this pilot corrects those labels consistently, while preserving the historical 105-question results.
  • All available raw history is retained in this pilot; the current overview is extra input, so it does not reduce transcript or audio length.
  • Every earlier detailed-note and overview call is charged once per meeting and speaker condition up to its last evaluated request, including invalid edits and unused updates; input and generated-token costs remain separate.
  • Each job used one A100 SXM4 80 GB, with model weights, audio encoder where used and an 8 GiB KV cache on GPU 0; writer and answer stages shared a job-private vLLM engine, with no shared service or TTS.
  • Text and audio jobs used two independent lanes with at most two GPUs active; all pilot jobs are complete, and this report update uses zero GPUs.
  • Final pilot, probe and input-order jobs used 3.025 GPU hours; including earlier prompt-development attempts raises this to 3.391 GPU hours, excluding the historical generation of reused detailed notes.
  • The source cutoffs are causal, while real-time completion of background writing remains untested; first-text time is measured from the question-time model call, with audio loading and preprocessing included for audio models.

5. Inputs and saved notes

  • Reactive text S2 receives the complete available speaker-attributed reference transcript and the participant’s question.
  • Current notes: the transcript and question plus one current state of generated facts, owner/status labels, cited source passages and retained changes.
  • Source quotes: the transcript and question plus the original passages referenced in that note state. The extra view excludes generated facts, note IDs, owner/status labels and links.
  • Quotes + earlier evidence: the same passages plus earlier source IDs from versions of still-active notes before the saved-note cutoff. Current passages have priority within a 4,096-token quote budget; some older passages may be omitted.
  • A shared Qwen3-30B-A3B Instruct model generates notes for both text thinkers from each new 25–40 second transcript segment, ten seconds of recent context and one current note state.
  • Audio S2 receives the past recording and spoken question, either alone or with labelled voice examples drawn only from earlier speech.
  • Each audio model generates its own notes from the new audio segment, recent audio and one current note state; it does not receive the future question or a reference transcript.
  • All note updates use the same kinds of functions. The edit history retains earlier versions, while the current view is bounded.

The fixed schedule groups meaningful speech into segments; silence and a very short final remainder need not cause a note update. Audio encoders can divide these segments into smaller windows without changing their content or thinking boundaries. The text answerer receives the full available transcript and, in a saved-material condition, one note-derived view; Reactive receives no saved material. This is offline causal replay: the writer never sees the future question, and the final call selects only notes from before the question’s insertion time. Real-time availability of unfinished notes remains a separate test.

Source quotes test whether highlighting note-selected transcript passages helps while omitting generated note text. The writer chooses source IDs before the question; the program copies the associated utterances without question-based search or relevance ranking. These utterances already appear in the full transcript, so the extra view repeats evidence rather than adding new meeting speech. Source IDs can be wrong or uninformative.

The writer makes note edits after transcript segments. The question-time text prompt contains the full transcript plus one selected saved view; it does not interleave each segment with a saved S2 thinker reasoning block. In the Thinking condition, request-time reasoning is generated after the question.

Worked example: how a transcript passage becomes a source quote

In meeting EN2003a, the later question asks how adding a poster session changed the number of talks. The transcript available to every condition already includes a speaker stating an earlier limit of about 36 talks, a later statement of 30 talks, and a passage about reserving a morning or afternoon for posters.

  1. Before the question, the note writer receives a transcript segment with utterance IDs and the current saved notes. It chooses source IDs when it adds or updates a note; it does not receive the future question.
  2. One active note, N13, is about running two talks in a row and cites EN2003a-U00217, the utterance about the earlier limit of 36 talks. Another, N15, has owner A, status agreed and version 2. Its generated fact says “The absolute limit on total talks is about thirty-six, … with at least six posters expected.” It cites EN2003a-U00254, the utterance that says the group could have thirty talks. These are saved note text and citations, not a guarantee that the cited words support the note.
  3. For Source quotes, the program copies the transcript utterances in the current note state’s source list. In this example that includes U00217 and U00254, alongside 34 other passages. Those passages are repeated in the extra saved material; they were already in the full transcript. The generated note text, note IDs, owner/status labels and links are not in this source-only view.
  4. For Quotes + earlier evidence, the program also follows source IDs from earlier versions of still-active notes, subject to the quote budget. It adds 21 passages in this case, including EN2003a-U00255 about a morning or afternoon for posters, which an earlier version of note N14 cited. This passage was also already in the full transcript.

The program follows saved IDs; it does not use the question as a search query or rank passages by relevance to it. N13’s citation illustrates a limitation: its cited utterance states the total-talk limit, while the note concerns talks in a row. Checking that an ID exists does not establish that it supports the generated note.

Memory functions and event changes

add_note records a distinct fact; update_note changes the same task; delete_note removes a duplicate or error; link_notes relates events; no_change skips unchanged material. The program validates arguments and available source IDs before applying edits. The text writer allows one repair attempt for invalid edits; rejected audio operations remain unapplied. For example, a proposed Friday deadline can change to an agreed Monday deadline while retaining both source passages and the earlier version. Current notes are limited to 4,096 tokens, with eight recent changes.

Model context and timing

Qwen3-30B-A3B Instruct and Thinking use reference transcripts. Audio models are MiniCPM-o 4.5 and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B Thinking. Qwen audio retains all history for every question. MiniCPM retains all history for 103/105 questions; its two longest inputs remove the oldest 303 or 219 seconds. Each model uses the same selected raw history across all its answer conditions.

Full-set jobs use one A100 SXM4 80 GB each, with model weights, the audio encoder when needed, and an 8 GiB KV cache on GPU 0. At most two independent jobs are active; the remaining Qwen questions are partitioned between jobs without changing per-answer settings. Model services are job-private and TTS is disabled. vLLM uses CUDA graphs and four concurrent note requests; controlled request-time answers are serial with cold prefix and audio caches. Instruct answers were batched, so their times are excluded from the latency comparison. Audio timing includes waveform loading and preprocessing.

6. Scoring

Required-fact correctness checks whether the answer supplies every fact requested by the question. Fully supported correctness also rejects material extra claims that the available transcript does not support. The evaluator receives the question, candidate answer, reference fact checklist and past transcript; it must quote the candidate’s words and cite supporting transcript IDs. Missing facts cannot be filled from the reference. Speaker identity can be expressed by a name, a speaker label or an unambiguous reference to an earlier utterance or audio segment.

Mechanical checks reject malformed judgments, unavailable transcript IDs and quotations absent from the candidate. Such cases await review rather than becoming wrong model answers. Empty final answers are scored wrong and retained. Native output tokens, thinking tokens and latency are collected independently of semantic scoring. MiniCPM final text ends at the first TTS end marker, following its official chat extractor; later control fragments are excluded from answer scoring while their generated-token costs remain counted.

The automatic scorer matched 265/315 fully supported labels in the source-reviewed control set. Its remaining labels are provisional. Codex source review corrects individual responses and is recorded with transcript IDs and reasons; the answer browser shows that status. 1192 saved outputs currently match source-reviewed responses.

In an additional check, Codex reviewed 36 responses without seeing their automatic labels; 31 fully supported labels agreed, and the disagreements were corrected.
