Questions in the requester’s voice.

The original participants did not ask them in the source recording. Answer generation is deferred. Synthesis is prepared offline; this page does not demonstrate live TTS latency. Voice and environment scores are automated checks, with perceptual matching left for listening review.

How the question audio is generated and inserted

Question generation and speech synthesis are separate stages. The text pipeline decides what a participant could ask from earlier meeting history. This pipeline uses a short original utterance to condition how that approved question sounds, then places its waveform into the recording.

  1. 1Choose the question events

    Take three unchanged, accepted questions from the text study: B asks about poster capacity and outreach; C asks about the webpage deadlines.

    Input
    Approved question text, requester ID and planned source time: B at 17:55.00 and 26:18.92; C at 35:35.50.
    Output
    Three question events that say what to speak, whose voice to condition on and where to pause the original recording. Reference-answer text is not a synthesis input.

  2. 2Prepare the speaker prompts

    Crop roughly 8–10 seconds of the requester’s earlier speech and retain its manual transcript. Compare individual-headset and mixed-channel references, plus an alternate utterance.

    Input
    Original AMI audio, manual word timing and the corrected speaker/channel mapping: B = Headset-1, C = Headset-3. Every conditioning excerpt precedes its question time.
    Output
    Short reference WAV + known words + sample rate. Keep a different original utterance for speaker-similarity checking; it never conditions the TTS model.

  3. 3Run zero-shot speaker-conditioned TTS

    Generate new speech in the reference speaker’s voice without training speaker-specific weights. Qwen and Chatterbox generate speech codes (audio tokens) for a neural decoder; F5 generates a mel-spectrogram (a time–frequency representation) for its Vocos vocoder.

    Input
    Question text + reference audio. Qwen3-TTS and F5-TTS also receive the reference transcript; Chatterbox uses the audio reference. Delivery and sampling settings are varied. Qwen inference uses vLLM-Omni.
    Output
    Raw question WAVs at 24 kHz: three models × three questions × four settings = 36 outputs, with the exact reference and settings logged. The TTS model receives a short voice prompt, not the full meeting history.

  4. 4Check words and compare voices

    Parakeet ASR transcribes each generated waveform. An ECAPA speaker encoder converts it and unused original utterances into speaker embeddings for comparison.

    Input
    Raw question WAV, intended question text, a held-out utterance from the requester and an utterance from the other requester.
    Output
    Recognized words and word error rate (WER), plus similarity to each speaker. Choose outputs with accurate spoken words first, then the strongest requester similarity within each model/question group: nine listening candidates.

  5. 5Match the recording and recheck audio

    Resample to the meeting’s 16 kHz, trim outer silence, match active-speech RMS level and add only missing background-noise power using earlier speech-free room tone. Short fades affect inserted audio only.

    Input
    Selected raw WAV, original same-speaker level in the meeting mix and captured room tone from before the insertion time.
    Output
    Nine processed question WAVs, rechecked with Parakeet, Whisper-base and speaker/clipping measurements. Final clips use no extra EQ or added reverberation. ASR and speaker scores support screening; naturalness and identity still need listening.

  6. 6Pause, splice and resume

    Copy original audio up to the selected source time, insert the processed question with short room-tone pads, then resume from the same original sample.

    Input
    Original mixed-headset waveform, source-time event and the processed question WAV. The source clock stays fixed while playback advances through the insertion.
    Output
    For review: about 14–20 seconds of preceding context, the synthetic question and 5 seconds of resumed source. Locally: a full replay with three F5 insertions and a 480 ms packet manifest. Original source samples are preserved exactly.

Side experiment: TTS → voice conversion

Input: the F5 deadline-question waveform and a target-speaker reference from C. Module: Chatterbox’s voice-conversion model keeps the spoken content while trying to change the voice. Output: two converted question WAVs, checked again with ASR and the speaker encoder. Both reduce C’s held-out similarity, so the main replay uses direct F5. The converted clips remain available below.

Two clocks, one pause. If the source pauses at 17:55, its next original sample still comes from 17:55 after the question finishes; playback time has advanced by the inserted duration. Each model-comparison clip is a separate branch from the original recording. The local full replay combines the three selected F5 questions. Neither produces an assistant answer or demonstrates live TTS latency.

Speech and evaluation terms used here
Speaker conditioning / zero-shot voice cloning
Use an example of a person’s speech to guide a new utterance in that voice, without fine-tuning model weights on that person.
Vocoder / neural audio decoder
The component that turns a mel-spectrogram or speech codes into waveform samples that can be played.
ASR and WER
Automatic speech recognition converts the synthesized audio back into text. Word error rate compares those recognized words with the intended question; zero WER is an ASR check, not proof of exact pronunciation.
Speaker embedding and cosine similarity
A numerical voice representation and a score comparing two utterances. The original comparison utterance is held out from TTS conditioning, but is used for development-time selection; the score is not an identity probability.
Active-speech RMS and room tone
Average waveform level while speech is active, and the recording’s low-level background noise. Matching them helps the inserted audio sit at the level and noise floor of the meeting.

What was tuned and checked

Thirty-six question-only outputs compare three architectures and four settings per question, followed by two voice-conversion controls. Model and setting selection uses independently decoded wording, followed by voice similarity to a separate original utterance. All nine selected processed WAVs have zero normalized Parakeet WER. The chosen examples preserve all original context PCM samples; a full local replay injects three F5 questions on a 480 ms packet timeline. F5 has the highest held-out cosine among wording-qualified variants for each question. VC lowers speaker C similarity from 0.571 to 0.496/0.403 and is excluded from the main replay.

Each model runs four configurations for each of three questions. The sweep compares headset versus mixed-channel conditioning, an alternate earlier utterance, and model-specific delivery controls. This is inference and reference tuning; no model weights were trained. Two VC controls are reported below and are excluded from the main replay.

This is a development pilot with three questions and two speakers from one meeting. ASR and speaker embeddings are proxies: they do not prove natural prosody, exact accent, perceived identity or a physical room match. The assistant inspected transcripts, timing and audio measurements; listening review is still requested from the user. Whisper-base hears Remami instead of remind me in the Chatterbox C clip while Parakeet recovers the intended words, so pronunciation remains uncertain there. Speaker C is comparatively harder. Tuning covers reference, inference and acoustic settings; model weights were not trained. No live synthesis or answer-generation result is claimed.

Runtime repairs and exact local recipe

The first Qwen attempt used the upstream offline example and produced failed outputs with a padded 2048-token prompt. The corrected caller supplies the reference codec-frame count used by the official serving implementation. All three successful sweeps ran on one L40S 48 GB each.
