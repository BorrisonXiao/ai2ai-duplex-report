How these clips are made

  1. Question selection. Input: the existing reviewed questions, requester identities and source times. Output: one spoken request per question, with the “Assistant,” cue. Codex preserves the question’s required facts.
  2. Speaker enrollment. Input: a short recording of the same person and its checked transcript. Output: the voice prompt for F5-TTS. Enrollment means giving TTS a voice example; it is not model training.
  3. Speech synthesis. Input: the spoken question and voice prompt. Output: candidate waveforms from the frozen F5-TTS v1 Base checkpoint and Vocos waveform decoder, using the existing fixed inference settings.
  4. Speech checks. Input: candidate waveforms and the intended question. Output: Parakeet transcripts and speaker-similarity scores. Check the whole question and wake word, then select among qualifying candidates. Three proper-name cases remain flagged for pronunciation review; their recordings are available but are not counted as word-accuracy passes. ECAPA is a speaker encoder; its cosine score measures embedding similarity and participates in selection, so it is not an independent human voice rating.
  5. Acoustic matching and insertion. Input: selected speech, the requester’s original recording level and a verified quiet gap in the preceding recording, when available. Output: level-matched speech with only missing background-noise power added, then a pause/question/resume clip. When no quiet gap can be verified, no background sound is added. No extra EQ or reverberation is applied. The new clips retain 18 seconds before the question and five seconds after it; the original PCM samples are copied exactly.
  6. Final verification. Input: the processed question waveform. Output: a second Parakeet check, an independent Whisper transcript and final speaker diagnostics. These are automatic checks; human naturalness and acoustic review remain open.

Voice enrollment is separate from conversation history. 11 new questions use conditioning speech recorded after the insertion time, as authorized for speakers with little earlier speech. Those clips and transcripts never enter the duplex model’s history or background preparation. Cards label these cases. Some comparison utterances also come from later speech and are for listening only. All 20 questions in the frozen six-meeting subset use earlier conditioning speech.

The recording clock stops during the synthetic question. The player clock continues. A listening excerpt is not evidence that a model can retain the full preceding meeting. Eight call insertions move slightly to an estimated word boundary; the original transcript cutoffs remain available in the data.
