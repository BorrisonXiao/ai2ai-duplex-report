Saving sound cues before a question

  • On four held-out muffled recordings, automatic sound timing raised supported answers from one to three. Answer comparison
  • Topic notes kept the score at three of four and reduced request-time thinking, but one correct answer gained and another was lost. Two examples
  • The thinker saved both listener-confirmed laughs and their topics, then flagged laughter in 7/15 separately annotated excerpts. Independent detection check

What the thinker heard

We tested Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking on AMI design meetings and ConEC earnings calls. Before anyone asked a question, the note writer heard each excerpt once. At question time, the thinker heard both excerpts in order and the spoken request. Each excerpt lasted at most 40 seconds.

  • Audio alone. The thinker answered immediately from the recording.
  • Broad meeting notes. A function call saved facts, commitments and any distinctive sounds that the thinker noticed.
  • Thinker listens for sounds. The writer specifically listened for laughter, muffled speech and chimes. It could save the sound, its start and end, the speaking turn and the nearby topic, or record that it heard none.
  • Automatic sound time. A CPU detector supplied a sound type, excerpt and estimated interval. A bandwidth check detected muffled speech, and YAMNet detected laughter.
  • Automatic time and topic note. For each detection, the thinker also heard that excerpt and saved a phrase identifying the speaking turn and what was being discussed.
  • Known edit time. For comparison, the answerer received the interval where we had applied the muffling edit.

Did the notes help answer the question?

Each controlled question has two recordings. We kept the words fixed and moved the muffling from one speaker to another. A correct answer identifies the indicated contribution and gives source-supported details. Both versions must pass to count a question as correct on both recordings.

On the four held-out recordings, mean request-time thinking fell from 297 tokens with automatic timestamps to 230 with timestamps and topic notes. The four topic-writing calls used another 7,439 generated tokens before the question, including a call that reached its limit without returning a note. Timestamp-only runs used the CPU detector and generated no earlier tokens. The mean saving came from the AMI recording. On ConEC, the topic note left request-time thinking unchanged for one version and added six tokens for the other.

The bandwidth detector localized 8/8 muffled edits in development and 4/4 held out. It also flagged 1/8 and 0/4 unedited companion excerpts, respectively. Each hit required at least 25% intersection over union with the edit interval. The sound-focused Qwen writer localized 1/8 development edits and flagged muffling on two unedited excerpts. It did not qualify for the held-out muffling comparison. If “posts and talks” is accepted for “posters and talks”, its development answers rise from 1/8 to 2/8.

Could the system hear natural laughter?

One listener confirmed the excerpt with laughter and its topic in an AMI meeting and an American Express interview. We used those two recordings for development. We then used laughter annotations on four other meetings and four other calls. Every 16-second excerpt containing a laughter tag had a nearby 16-second speech control from the same recording without a tag.

Audio alone and all four tested note conditions answered both listener examples correctly. The broad notes recorded topics but omitted the laughter twice. The sound-focused notes recorded both the laughter and its topic twice. Mean request-time thinking was 191 tokens for audio alone, 209 for broad notes, 206 for the sound-focused writer, 168 for automatic timing and 259 for automatic timing with topic notes. Two examples cannot establish a latency improvement.

What the notes actually said

A meeting introduction

In the second AMI excerpt the group is laughing as a participant asks why someone is looking at them. Before hearing the request, the thinker saved both the speaking turn and its connection to the introductions.

Saved note

Why are you looking at me? · role and animal relation in the project

Answer

The laughter occurs in excerpt 2 between 9.5 and 11.5 seconds, during a discussion about the speakers' roles and how their animals relate to the project.

Airline miles on the American Express call

In the first excerpt, the host hoped to use his Delta miles and said his family would appreciate it. The sound-focused writer identified the speaking turn by its words.

Saved note

I think my family would appreciate that. · Delta miles

Answer

The laughter occurred in excerpt 1 between 5.5 and 6.5 seconds while the speaker was discussing using Delta miles and how his family would appreciate them.

A note corrected a speaker mix-up

In a held-out AMI meeting, the timestamp-only answer called the industrial designer Chloe. The topic note identified her as Katie, and the final answer gave the correct contribution.

Saved note

Katie · presenting about the inter workings of the remote control

Answer

She was going to present about the inter workings of the remote control.

A note changed a financial term

The analyst asked about decremental margins. Given the timestamp alone, the thinker used that term correctly. The topic note changed it to incremental, and the same error appeared in the final answer.

Saved note

Morning, maybe just a first question... · incremental margins and cost synergies phasing

Answer

The analyst wanted to understand the phasing of cost synergies through the year and whether the $40-50 million in other cost-out actions would cause incremental margins to narrow.
