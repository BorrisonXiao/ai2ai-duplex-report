What does the dataset contain?

A case is one question about one recording. A controlled question has two audio versions and counts once. The same laughter question appears on two different recordings and contributes two cases.

Table 1. All question–recording cases, including reserve questions and the two listener-reviewed examples.
Question typeAMIConECTotal
Semantic9740137
Controlled sound448
Natural laughter112
All cases10245147

Semantic questions ask about the words people spoke. Controlled sound questions use an edited chime or muffled voice to identify which remarks the question refers to. Natural laughter questions ask which excerpt contained laughter and what people were discussing.

Which questions are in the balanced evaluation?

We selected 88 questions for comparisons across corpora. The development and held-out questions come from different recordings within each corpus.

Table 2. Counts per corpus in the balanced set. Each row applies separately to AMI and to ConEC.

The inventory also includes 57 AMI semantic questions in reserve and 2 separate natural-laughter development cases, one from each corpus. For an equal-weight comparison, a 16-question subset of the balanced set contains four semantic and four controlled sound questions per corpus.

When are questions asked?

AMI averaged 16.5 min (46% through the recording) and ConEC averaged 30.2 min (60% through the recording). The 145 timed questions average 20.7 minutes from the start. Continuous previews use the source time where the speaker finishes and the recording pauses. Paired sound and semantic examples retain their experiment cutoff here. The appendix gives the exact source times of both excerpts. The two laughter examples also use paired excerpts.

Questions about an edited sound

Play either version. Both use the same two excerpts and the same requester’s question. Moving the muffling or chime changes which remarks answer the question. F5-TTS uses a separate voice sample from each requester. We match their speaking level and mix in background sound from the recording. In both demos we use noise with the same level and frequency profile as the quietest 5% of the earlier recording. For AMI, the first attempt at using recorded room tone produced recognizable words in the background check. The earlier sound-cue experiments used a stock Flite voice.
