What a valid question must require

The same real AMI recording supplies all four source prefixes. The current requesters are B and C; A remains part of their source conversation. A synthetic requester is one of A, B or C, addressing a separate meeting assistant. The source pauses for one independent question/answer branch. EN2003a is a workshop-planning meeting with no formal job-role labels in its metadata; the displayed participant perspectives are inferred from cited earlier speech.

A valid reference must be supported by completed available speech before the question. A useful history test must need an older fact that the last minute alone cannot supply. Old citations, fluent answers and a reader saying “answerable” do not establish that requirement. Review also checks natural wording, attribution, tentative versus settled plans and answers accidentally revealed by the question.

Available transcript chunks use manual word timing plus an assumed half-second sentence-release delay. Unfinished sentences can therefore contain audible words not yet in the text prefix. This conservative preparation convention is not streaming ASR. Source start/end times remain visible so overlapping speech is not mistaken for sequential turns.

The completed pilot contains 48 parsed drafts: 40 local-model drafts and eight assistant-authored drafts. All were reviewed. The final set has nine variants: six authored questions, two unchanged 30B deadline paraphrases of one intent, and one verbatim Instruct question with its reference pruned by the assistant. The table counts only raw draft decisions; the edited reference is a separate curated record, not an unchanged model pass.

The initial 16 generation calls requested 32 questions but returned 22 parsed candidates. Three 4B calls and two 30B calls hit their output cap. Higher-cap v3 returned all 16 requested questions without truncation; none passed the strong-history review. Increasing the cap solved completion in this run and did not solve its semantic defects.

The graph-enabled v3 measured 187.05 output tokens/s for 4B Instruct and 166.82 for 30B Thinking across its generation calls; initial eager runs measured about 84.53 and 32.66. Output lengths, prompts and allowances also differ, so these are observed call rates rather than a controlled speedup estimate. Thinking output counts include private reasoning.

Assistant-selected evidence packets reduced generator inputs to at most 925 tokens. The 4B model returned only one question per call instead of two; two outputs were malformed JSON. Their final text is preserved and inspected, without silent repair. One parsed deadline question was retained with a reference-only edit removing unrequested technical-helper content. This setting did not establish reliable automatic question generation.

The final 1,024-token bounded-citation reader parsed all 192 responses, eliminating the earlier JSON truncation problem. It still gives a wrong capacity-revision answer and confuses outreach ownership/audiences despite declaring those answers answerable. The webpage pairs each accepted question with an explicit content review of its reader response; question validity is grounded in source evidence rather than reader success. Fifteen final reader responses cite IDs outside their supplied context; those remain explicit validation flags.

Two authored drafts were excluded after checking short-history sufficiency. Retained questions need case-specific earlier facts, but one question-only technical-requirements answer is generic and unsupported rather than an abstention. Required answer facts and paraphrase guidance are separated from optional reference detail. The two 30B deadline questions and edited Instruct question are paraphrases of one intent, not independent benchmark samples.

All three GPU jobs completed sequentially on one H100 SXM 80 GB at a time: 1110768 and 1110785 load 4B/30B generators and a 4B reader; 1111068 uses only 4B generation/reading on GPU 0. CPU audit 1111132 uses zero GPUs. Actual combined allocation is 0.668889 GPU hours. No experiment GPU remains active. No TTS or speech-model replay was run; source roles are inferred from prior speech, not formal EN2003a metadata.

What existing pipelines contribute

PREME provides the closest inspected meeting-question generation pipeline: split discussion into topics, sample questions and evaluate answerability and coverage. Roundtrip consistency contributes answer reconstruction with the proposed answer withheld. Meta’s Synthetic Data Kit supplies a practical chunk/generate/curate implementation. The timed cutoff and requester motivation are additional design choices here; scripted synthetic QA itself is established practice.

Convert approved text into an audio replay

Question text and grounded reference facts come first. A later replay can assign a consistent synthetic voice to each requester and a separate assistant voice, pause the source recording at its original timestamp, insert the question and let the tested model answer. Generic TTS voices do not sound like the original AMI speakers, so the first condition needs explicit requester identity. Voice-only identity recovery is a separate experiment.

Question WAVs are model inputs. Optional reference-answer WAVs are listening examples, never model inputs or unique acoustic scoring targets. Independent transcription should check words, numbers and names. Source time stays fixed during the inserted exchange while playback time advances. Each request starts from its own source prefix; synthetic answers do not silently become later context. No TTS has been generated and no live speech-model behavior is claimed by this text pilot.
