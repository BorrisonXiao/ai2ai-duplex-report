Useful questions earlier in the conversation

Call on the assistant explicitly

Every current question begins with the spoken cue “Assistant,”. It is part of the question text and the regenerated audio, and both ASR systems recover it in all ten selected clips. The requester keeps their original voice. This cue clearly calls on the separate assistant; reliable triggering and response timing still require a live duplex-model test.

How the questions and audio are made

Who does what? Codex selects sources, writes some questions and reviews the results. Local Qwen models generate the other questions and answer questions from transcripts as a quality check. The meeting assistant is the duplex model that would hear and answer the inserted question.

EN2002b is an AMI project meeting about building a meeting browser. ES2002b, IS1000b and TS3003b are AMI role-play meetings about designing a remote control. Danaher call 4344338 and Monro call 4320211 add technical financial discussion. Every request addresses a separate assistant. Earnings-call requests simulate a private aside while listening.

Speaker diarization identifies who spoke; this pilot uses AMI’s manual speaker labels. Time alignment locates those words on the recording clock. Voice conditioning guides synthesis with a short requester recording without training new weights. ASR transcribes generated speech to check its words.

AMI question times fall in annotated gaps without cutting a word. Completed transcript excerpts become available after an assumed 0.5-second delay. Earnings timestamps come from automatic alignment and support text controls here; their audio insertion points remain unverified.

What did the generation pilot produce?

The 30B Qwen3 Instruct model produced 78 complete drafts. Codex wrote 36 distinct questions, and the local model contributed seven more. Eight additional saved texts are alternative phrasings of these same questions. The model wrote useful ordinary reminders more reliably than questions needing a second source fact. Codex wrote the six strongest source-linked candidates after inspecting the passages.

The initial run compared ordinary reminders with a connected-fact prompt, which returned no connected questions. Codex then selected six related source pairs and tested composition and independent rewriting. A shorter prompt used verified fact cards and three unrelated writing examples. The setting called “Tell the model to ask the meeting assistant” says the request is for the separate assistant to recall the discussion; it should not ask that assistant to finish a participant’s work or make a new promise. That prompt clarified who should answer, but did not require a spoken name. Rewriting receives an actual earlier simple question, without the composition method’s final spoken text.

When would the questions be inserted?

Counting each of the 43 distinct questions once, the mean insertion time is 13.56 minutes after the start, or 36.25% of the conversation. Thirty-one questions use early checkpoints, whose actual times range from 4:56 to 10:01. Later checkpoints supply longer-context examples.

We prepared 24 cutoffs: near five, seven-and-a-half and ten minutes, plus a later point, in each conversation. Retained questions use 23 positions. Choosing a gap can move the target: ES2002b’s nominal ten-minute point is 9:16. Multiple questions at one point are alternatives for separate trials. Fractions use official AMI meeting durations and pinned full-call durations; the recording clock includes setup pauses.

The first retained representative supplies the time for each question. The eight alternative phrasings and any additional test times remain available on the cards, without reweighting these statistics.

Why the count changed from 39 topic groups to 43 distinct questions

The earlier topic grouping combined four genuinely different controls with broader questions. For example, asking for the current audience is different from asking how the audience changed. We split those four groups by the facts requested; no new question was generated for this recount.

Does the reader need the earlier context?

Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 receives the question and transcript, without proposed answers, selected source lists or generation rationale. These are offline transcript-reader tests, separate from a live speech assistant.

  • Full history: all speech available at the original question time.
  • Latest minute: only the preceding sixty seconds.
  • Selected evidence removed: omit cited passages; other mentions may remain.
  • Connected-fact controls: remove each fact group from the full history, then try each group alone. Repeated mentions and guessing from a single remaining speaker can weaken this test.
  • Growing context: eight early questions with stable targets are tested again at later transcript cutoffs. These offline repetitions measure distraction from accumulating context.

Counting one fixed representative for each of the 43 distinct questions, 39 full-history responses recovered the main requested facts. The 20 later replays of eight stable early questions also retained their main facts. These checks used question bodies without the spoken Assistant cue; extra claims and citations can still be wrong.

One full-history answer, about unsent meeting notes in IS1000b, hit its 1536-token response limit before completing the structured output. It counts as a failed main answer, rather than an omitted test.

The extra fact controls cover eight questions: six authored source links, one authored January-versus-quarter comparison and one model evidence comparison. Removing each group from the complete history and trying each group alone produces 32 responses.

No spoken text is certified as requiring multiple reasoning steps from reader failure alone. Some fact-removal masks leave related mentions elsewhere, and a context containing just one person or feature can make an unsupported guess easy. These small controls expose weaknesses in both the reader and the proposed dependency tests.

Matching citation IDs to the supplied context checks only that those IDs exist. It does not establish that their words support the answer. For example, the reader correctly answered thirty-four percent for learning difficulty in IS1000b but cited the different discussion about repetitive-strain concerns.

Growing-context results use the original question spoken text at later cutoffs solely as offline controls. Their main facts stayed correct in this eight-question sample; three of the twenty replay responses nevertheless contained invalid citation IDs. This does not establish general long-context robustness.

These reader tests used 51 saved question texts covering 43 distinct questions, without the spoken Assistant cue. They are historical text controls. The current audio clips include the cue; duplex response timing has not been tested here.

Representative successes and failures

P-TS-dock and its paired simple control

In TS3003b, the survey said fifty percent of people lost their remote. Later, marketing proposed a docking-station button that makes the remote beep so it can be found. The linked question asks how common the problem addressed by that proposal was. Full history produced thirty percent, while the simple lost-remote question produced the correct fifty percent. Removing the percentage passage made the linked reader identify the missing percentage.

P-TS-target-change

The full-history reader recovered the change from marketing’s recommended ages sixteen to forty-five to the later forty-plus requirement. In reduced contexts it also invented or reconstructed age ranges from incomplete evidence, showing why a correct-looking answer alone does not prove the needed source link.

M-EC4320211-P10-reminder-1

Monro described its California conversions as substantially completed and still planned to finish them. Asked whether they were fully rebranded, the reader began with yes. The qualification in the source must remain part of the answer.

P-EN-report

The reader correctly recalled the proposed report section, changes since the initial specification, but cited shortened IDs absent from the input. The source-content answer and citation-format failure are recorded separately.

An empty-history application guard returns missing context before invoking a model. That CPU control is excluded from model accuracy counts.

Listen to requester-matched questions

Ten selected AMI questions cover four meetings and eight requester voices. Six use early checkpoints. Each voice has past-only conditioning speech and disjoint earlier comparison speech. The cue follow-up generated 30 F5-TTS outputs using three settings per question, then four slower versions of the timeline question to clarify its opening cue. The final timeline clip uses direct slower speech. Model weights and past-only voice references are unchanged.

Selection checks spoken text first, then a held-out speaker embedding: a numerical voice-similarity proxy. Speech is resampled to sixteen kilohertz and matched to the requester’s active speech level, with room tone from a preceding gap. Main clips have zero added equalization and no added reverberation. A strict word-error rate is the number of word insertions, deletions and substitutions divided by the target word count, ignoring case and punctuation. The cards also show a separate CPU Whisper transcription. The ASR models sometimes disagree; please judge the spoken text, voice and acoustics by listening.

The source clock follows the original meeting. The playback clock includes the inserted question. Main players include eighteen seconds of preceding audio and five seconds of continuation. Original samples on either side are copied exactly. The original participants did not record these synthetic questions.

Ten question-insertion clips are ready for listening. All ten include the spoken Assistant cue in both ASR systems. Eight match the full target text exactly in Parakeet after case and punctuation normalization; one has a spoken contraction and one has a filler-word disagreement between ASR systems. All ten are closer to their own held-out voice than to the other tested requester in the same meeting.
