Earlier timing and content design, retained for later work

The two questions we want the experiments to answer

When to think?

Decide whether a newly observed event is worth private computation now, and when to stop, cancel or update that work.

What to think?

Choose the useful job: retain a responsibility, track a changed decision, resolve a reference, check a constraint, or derive a simple consequence.

Speaker-attributed ASR is an input representation: it helps preserve who said what. The amortized thinking task decides what to do with that information. Correct transcription/diarization is not itself useful reasoning.

Recommendation: First keep the original three-question/four-condition comparison as a plumbing and history check. Then separate schedule and work-content experiments, followed by a small crossed comparison. Start with controllers and verified notes; do not train a policy yet.

There is close prior work. LTS-VoiceAgent (Zou et al., 2026) already combines semantic thought triggers and maintained state. Proactive Thinking (Wang et al., 2026) prepares for predicted user replies. GroupMemBench (Yang et al., 2026) shows why preserving speaker-specific information matters. Our proposed emphasis is a controlled multi-speaker experiment with causal inputs, revisions and complete cost accounting; this is a design direction, not a novelty claim.

Conditions that keep the comparison interpretable

  • Keep future questions hidden. The amortized thinking process receives only released speech and prior notes. It does not receive question text, insertion schedule, answer labels or selected evidence. A guessed future question must be generated from the past and charged as speculation.
  • Version the ASR and speaker labels. Use only words and speaker assignments emitted by that instant. Later transcript/identity corrections are new events. Oracle speaker labels attached to ASR must be labeled as an upper-bound condition, not a causal diarizer.
  • Separate amortized thinking from speaking. The six ordinary-discussion controls allow reasoning while listening while requiring no outward speech. The real Assistant wake cue permits a response; a pause or thought trigger does not.
  • Count the complete cost. Include ASR, speaker attribution, gating, all prompt/prefill and generated/private tokens, note storage, invalidated/cancelled calls and unused work. Report allocated GPU hours separately from active inference/service-time proxies.
  • Measure useful audible answers. Time from question end to the first correct useful fact and to a complete supported answer. Also report deadline success over every case, wrong answers, silence, cancellations, native STOP events, S1 queue lag and audio dropouts. An acknowledgement does not count as a useful fact.
  • Control models and costs. Hold the same S2 model in all cells. Use shared budget caps, plot quality and delay against measured cost, and compare overlapping cost ranges before claiming a timing benefit. Token-matched runs can still spend different compute on reading history.
  • Qualify retention and integration first. Separate S1 keeping up with audio from S2 remembering earlier facts. Start bounded, test older-fact access as history grows, verify processor token/audio limits and enough KV memory, then extend. A preloaded history replay is a useful controlled diagnostic, not proof of full 18–35-minute listening.
  • Report the small-sample limit. The first three questions are case studies. Variants of the same question do not add independent cases. Use paired outcomes and meeting-level summaries; six meetings cannot establish general policy superiority.

Before submitting any speech inference

  1. Use source text copied by code rather than asking the model to reproduce transcript quotes. Check whether the summarized claim follows from it; exact source text alone does not prove the claim.
  2. Separate who spoke, who owns the task, and who endorsed a proposal. The initial single speaker field was insufficient to prevent ownership mistakes.
  3. Retain durable rules/constraints until contradicted, instead of keeping only the latest 32 notes; compare alternatives under the same memory cap.
  4. Check note completion under the chosen token cap on development meetings. In a Thinking model the cap includes private reasoning; an empty or unfinished note is a budget/completion failure, not evidence against a timing policy.
  5. Audit the source meaning of updates before testing timing. The initial notes missed useful facts and sometimes upgraded proposals or lost uncertainty.
  6. Qualify the larger S2 context/KV allocation and S1 rolling audio/history behavior. No current inference config has been changed.
  7. Keep a genuinely new meeting split for a later confirmatory policy evaluation if these pilot results are used repeatedly to refine prompts or gates.

Current capacity and the cost of a first replay

  • Existing S1/S2 examples establish selected short interactions; the current S1 configuration keeps 40 history turns with a 4096-token window.
  • Current deployed S2 configuration uses an 8192-token window. A full meeting prefix cannot be silently truncated to fit it.
  • The original three full-transcript S2 inputs need 11251, 16554 and 22500 input tokens before reserving 4096 output tokens. None fits the current 8192 configuration; a 32768 window needs an estimated 4.5 GiB BF16 KV for one sequence, before other runtime memory.
  • Extending the oracle-transcript check to all 24 text cases reaches 37,309 input tokens; with 4,096 output tokens it requires a 49,152-token context, with an estimated 6.75 GiB single-sequence BF16 KV.

Resource proposal: For a future integrated replay, propose 2 H100 SXM 80 GB: S1 Thinker, Talker, MTP and Code2Wav share GPU 0; the existing Qwen3-4B Thinking S2 reasoner is isolated on GPU 1. ASR/speaker traces are produced in a separate sequential job and replayed from CPU for controlled comparisons, explicitly distinct from live ASR. The 30B reader remains an offline quality check. No such job is submitted.

CPU caller/history/cancellation checks and S2 context preflight before any new speech inference; measured S1 latency/queue qualification under S2 load before interpreting behavior.

At 1× playback the three original prefixes total 79.82 minutes per condition. Reserving two GPUs for each of the four system conditions would require at least 10.64 GPU hours before startup, request/answer time and ASR processing. A one-GPU S1-only allocation reduces that bound; all actual allocation and amortized thinking still count.

A cheaper first test: A cheaper first schedule/work study can use one isolated S2 worker and a causal text-event replay, skipping idle waits with a virtual clock. Label readiness/latency as simulated until selected two-GPU paced-audio cases validate it. A preloaded-history or bounded-window replay tests that declared setup; it does not prove continuous full-meeting listening.

Ready for discussion

  • Should reasoning while listening mainly maintain reliable facts, or also prepare likely answers? Start with facts/owners/status plus a few cheap derived checks. Keep speculative answer planning as an explicitly costed later control; it is less reliable when the future request is unknown.
  • What should count as a good time to think? Start with completed meaningful updates, compared with fixed intervals under the same caps. Acoustic silence is an opportunity for scheduling, not evidence that thought is useful.
  • How much amortized thinking should be allowed? Use a small predeclared cap and report the full cost curve, then adjust on development meetings only. Discuss the quality/latency/compute trade-off before a larger sweep.
  • What might eventually be learned? Learn a gate choosing whether to update/derive/verify now, and a work selector choosing the job and relevant facts. Train from source-checked state edits and measured utility on separate development meetings; do not imitate the quarantined free-form traces.
