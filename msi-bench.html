Definitions, caveats and sources

Coverage of the published evaluation protocol, not every task that could be built from the data. Model interface describes the intended input/output route; it does not imply that every capability of that model is evaluated.

Multi-party means a group setting, in text or audio. Synthetic describes the conversation/voice material; Mixed combines generated and human material. Target model: Text = text input/output; Audio→text = audio input, text/JSON output; Voice = voice interaction; Duplex = concurrent listening and speaking. Alternative adapters and text controls are listed below.

Content = semantic response scoring; Tools = call correctness; Timing = participation or response timing; User interruption = yielding/recovery when a user interrupts; Backchannels = generation or tolerance of brief acknowledgments; Speech quality = acoustic/prosodic scoring. A Yes does not imply comprehensive coverage. MSI bystander interference is distinct from directed user interruption. Source check: 2026-10-19.

MSI-Bench

Multi-speaker setting
Yes: 2–3 human roles plus assistant; some foreground/background speech overlaps.
Data origin
Synthetic scripts and cloned-voice TTS; recorded ambience and sound effects are mixed in. Not natural group conversations.
Input, protocol and model interface
Fixed audio cases → normalized action, answer text and optional calls. Core scoring supports audio-to-text/JSON models and speech/duplex adapters; transcript controls also exist.
Semantic response scoring
Yes: atomic criteria for memory, authority, disclosure, ownership, scope and constraints; judge reads normalized text/calls.
Tool-call scoring
Yes: semantic call criteria plus separate strict name/count/argument validation. Scores proposed calls, not real service execution.
Duplex and timing coverage
Limited: premature-response probes and bystander interference during an answer; live on duplex targets, approximated on turn-based targets.
What the scores do not establish
No general directed-interruption repair/stop-delay, backchannel-generation, pause/transition-timing or speech-fluency/quality suite. Core answer scores alone do not demonstrate duplex skill.

These are synthesized scripts

The benchmark authors report that the dialogue voices are synthesized from cloned Mozilla Common Voice reference clips. They report no real conversations in the release. Each case WAV also uses the scene’s listed ambience, and a background voice may overlap the foreground turn.

A stock update is spoken across the restaurant

A staff member calls out an item that is almost sold out and what will replace it. Maggie later asks the assistant to order by referring to that background speech.

Case: msi-en-retr-commerce-00 · 2 speakers · 6 WAV-backed turns in full case · 3 candidate functions listed in case metadata · 1 required calls · policy: exact_multiset · source case JSON

Turn 5 · Maggie to assistant10.7 s · 24 kHz mono PCM16

Skip it. But whatever I end up getting, swap the fries for the roasted carrots — and remind me to ask about a glass of the house red, the Malbec.

Overlapping background voice in this WAV: Marco says “After those go, we push the cider-glazed pork shank instead.” from about 1.55 to 5.55 seconds.

Turn 8 · Maggie to assistant14.2 s · 24 kHz mono PCM16

Oh shoot, I'm meeting someone at seven — no time to dither. Quick, put me down for the one they just said is nearly gone over by the counter, and keep whatever they're switching to in your back pocket in case I'm too late.

Expected behavior

Assistant action: respond. Make exactly the required calls listed below; no extra calls.

  1. place_order
    • dish = "braised_short_rib"
    • fallback_dish = "cider_glazed_pork_shank"
    • side = "roasted_carrots"

Dataset reference response: On it — I'm putting you down for the braised short rib, since that's the one Marco just called out as being down to the last couple of plates, with the roasted carrots in place of the fries like you asked. And if it's already gone by the time your order lands, the kitchen said they're switching to the cider-glazed pork shank, so I've got that queued as your backup.

Judge instruction: Assess whether the answer resolves the commerce / service assistant eavesdropping case. Credit only settled actions with correct holder, scope, temporal, and conflict bindings.

View the 4 atomic scoring criteria
  1. tool.call args

    prediction.tool_calls must semantically match these gold calls: [{"arguments":{"dish":"braised_short_rib","fallback_dish":"cider_glazed_pork_shank","side":"roasted_carrots"},"name":"place_order"}]. Allow meaning-preserving paraphrases in free-text arguments; function names, call multiplicity, numeric/categorical values, ownership bindings, and absence of extra calls must still be correct.

  2. response.required behavior

    The answer identifies the braised short rib as the nearly-sold-out dish Maggie is ordering and keeps the cider-glazed pork shank as the backup if the short rib runs out, using the details overheard from the distant counter talk.

  3. eavesdropping.foreground decoy rejection

    The answer does not substitute a foreground or unrelated detail for the overheard stock update — e.g., it must not treat the Malbec, the table bread, a starter, review-based menu talk, or the bystander's 'pickup point' remark as the dish that is nearly gone or as the basis for the order. An answer that merely fails to recover the dish, without adopting one of these decoys, does not fail this atom.

  4. eavesdropping.miss admission over fabrication

    If the answer does not recover the overheard dish or the substitute, it passes only by explicitly admitting it didn't catch what the staff said and asking Maggie to confirm (e.g., check with Marco); it fails if it names a fabricated dish or substitute as if overheard, or confirms/places the order while silently omitting the uncaught detail. An answer that correctly recovers and uses the overheard details passes automatically.

Inspect the model prompt, tool menu and normalized output

What the task prompt tells the model

This is a plain-language summary of the pinned turn-based prompt template. It directs the model to decide whether the latest speaker is addressing the assistant; answer or stay silent accordingly; use only available functions for supported external actions; and pair any calls with a brief spoken pre-action sentence that does not claim success. It asks for a structured response. The prompt does not ask the model to expose private reasoning. For this case, setup identifies the classroom-assistant role and date (May 25, 2026). The template says to bind S1/S2 to voices by first appearance when no participant map is supplied; in this dialogue, Xiaolin speaks first and Awei second. The runner sends human WAVs as separate chronological audio messages rather than concatenating them into one clip.

What the model and judge receive

The public runner turns a model’s response into a normalized prediction record. For the roommate case, expand “Inspect the model prompt, tool menu and normalized output” in that case card to see the available functions and a clearly labeled format example.

The judge receives the text and calls together

The evaluator passes the judge three named JSON values: manifest, prediction and rubric. The manifest view contains the scene, expected action, interaction-pattern label, any contract field present, and dialogue lines with speech-control/acoustic markup removed. The prediction view contains predicted_action, answer_text, tool_calls and confidence when present. The rubric contains its judge instruction and atomic criteria (polarity, dimension and criterion). For a call criterion, the gold calls are written into that criterion; the gold validator specification is not passed as a separate field.

The judge prompt explicitly says to evaluate prediction.answer_text and prediction.tool_calls against the criteria, and not to re-judge predicted_action. So the text and the call list are judged as parts of the same normalized prediction, not as a whole JSON string matched against the reference response. The answer text is judged semantically, so equivalent wording can pass. The judge sees the dialogue as labeled text lines, not the WAVs or generated speech audio. MSI-Bench reports no spoken-audio quality score. A no_calls sample still has its text response judged; the calls array should be empty.

There is also a separate deterministic tool validator. It checks required function names, argument values, call multiplicity and extra calls (order-independent under the sample’s exact_multiset policy). This validator does not send its verdict to the LLM judge; its pass/fail contributes separately to the tool metric and, for tool-required cases, the paper’s All-Pass decision. Invalid model JSON/schema is recorded as a format error rather than semantic success.

How the model is instructed: the turn-based template tells it to choose respond or silent. A response that needs an external action should include only supported calls from the offered menu and a short pre-action spoken sentence; it should not claim that an action succeeded. In prompt_json mode, action, text and calls are emitted in one JSON object. In native-tool mode, the provider returns tool calls through its function-call interface and the adapter combines those with the action/text fields into the same normalized record. For generated turn-based targets, the runner defaults OpenAI and Gemini to native declarations; other targets default to the text/JSON catalog unless configured otherwise. The paper also says open-weight targets use vLLM JSON-schema constrained decoding, while hosted targets use their public APIs.

The actual prompt placement depends on the adapter: some requests place the instruction in a system message, while some local chat templates place it at the start of the user message. Full-duplex adapters use a separate live prompt/event path. The pinned source exposes the benchmark’s own instructions and adapter requests; it does not expose vendor-internal hidden prompts or private reasoning layers.

What the released cases and paper show

The six case patterns form three balanced families. The paper reports mean All-Pass Rate across 15 evaluated model configurations by pattern.

Table 3. Mean pattern All-Pass Rate across the paper’s 15 evaluated model configurations; pattern values follow the displayed order.
Capability familyPatternsMean APR by pattern
Instruction followingSpeaker authority · Selective disclosure48.9% · 46.7%
ReasoningConstraint prioritization · Sequential constraint integration36.8% · 27.6%

In the paper, Gemini 3.1 Pro reaches the highest reported all-rubric pass rate on English (66.8%), while GPT Realtime 2.1 (xhigh) leads on Mandarin (54.5%). The strongest open-weight configuration, Qwen3-Omni-30B, reaches 34.0% and 19.3% on those splits. All-Pass Rate counts a case only when every atomic rubric passes, including the deterministic tool-call check where one applies. These are paper results, not a local evaluation in this workspace. See the paper for model settings, uncertainty intervals and full scores.

How to read the pattern gap

In the paper’s 15-configuration average, instruction-following patterns score highest, reasoning patterns fall in the middle, and memory patterns score lowest. Background-speech retrieval is the hardest pattern in that average. This is a pattern-level summary of the paper’s model runs, not a claim that every retrieval example is harder than every other case.
